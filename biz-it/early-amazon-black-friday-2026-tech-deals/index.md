## How to Spot Inflated MSRPs and Fake Markdowns

The modern discount marketplace relies heavily on anchored reference pricing. Retailers often restore an item to its original manufacturer suggested retail price (MSRP) for two weeks in October so they can display a dramatic "-40%" badge on November 11.

The [Federal Trade Commission Guides Against Deceptive Pricing](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/rules/guides-against-deceptive-pricing) prohibit companies from advertising fictitious previous prices to create the illusion of a bargain. Yet automated dynamic pricing algorithms exploit loopholes by fluctuating third-party merchant tags.

Before hitting the buy button, run the URL through browser extensions like Keepa or Camelcamelcamel. If an item advertised at "45% off" spent the entire months of July, August, and September selling at that exact sale price, it is not a holiday deal; it is standard street price disguised behind a red promotional badge.

Be particularly wary of consumer tech bundles. Third-party vendors regularly package an older, heavily discounted laptop with a $10 generic sleeve and mouse, marketing it as an exclusive bundle to bypass algorithmic price matching and disguise modest underlying discounts. Tracking individual model identifiers ensures you evaluate the computer on its actual silicon merits, especially when evaluating ecosystem-specific hardware from the [apple](/apple/) line.

## Hold Out or Check Out? The Final Call

If the item on your holiday radar is a display panel, a core laptop upgrade, or core networking infrastructure, waiting until November 27 introduces inventory risk for negligible return. Premium electronics with tight manufacturing yields rarely receive secondary cuts beneath current distributor margins. If the product is sitting at its 90-day historical floor today, checking out now saves both budget uncertainty and backorder shipping delays.

Hold off, however, on smaller budget accessories. Cables, chargers, low-tier wireless earbuds, external solid-state storage, and first-party Amazon streaming hardware follow aggressive volume-clearance strategies. Those sub-$100 categories will see steeper, algorithmic price wars in the final 72 hours leading up to Cyber Monday. For the heavy computing silicon, the holiday deals window has effectively arrived.
