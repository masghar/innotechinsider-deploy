# What Pakistan's first Tiangong experiments will test: algae and quinoa

> SUPARCO says Zeeshan Ali's science work includes growing microalgae and exposing quinoa seeds to spaceflight. Few details beyond that have been published.

By InnotechInsider Staff · Science · Nov 27, 2026
Canonical URL: https://innotechinsider.com/science/pakistan-tiangong-experiments-microalgae-quinoa-seeds/

Pakistan's first astronaut did not fly to Tiangong only to be photographed. Muhammad Zeeshan Ali is carrying joint Chinese-Pakistani experiments, and the public description of them is short.

## What SUPARCO has said

Citing Pakistan's space agency SUPARCO, [Dawn reports](https://www.dawn.com/news/2046753) two lines of work: cultivating microalgae as a possible source of food and oxygen for future missions, and testing the effects of spaceflight on quinoa seeds. Dawn also says Pakistani science payloads will begin in the station's Wentian and Mengtian lab modules, per SUPARCO's astronaut website. The first idea is a standard one in life-support research, since algae can turn light and carbon dioxide into biomass and oxygen. The second is a plant-breeding question: whether radiation and microgravity change seeds in ways that matter back on Earth.

## What is secondhand

[Space.com](https://www.space.com/space-exploration/human-spaceflight/pakistani-astronaut-becomes-1st-international-visitor-to-chinas-tiangong-space-station-photos) says Ali will conduct "a set of joint Chinese-Pakistani experiments," and relays a Times of India report of four collaborative projects in biotechnology and materials, plus an in-orbit experiment on photosynthetic cyanobacteria. Space.com attributes that list to the Indian paper rather than to an official briefing, so we are treating it as unconfirmed. Dawn also cites CMSA as saying the full Shenzhou-24 crew will carry out roughly 90 experiments during the mission, a figure that covers all crew members and not only Ali's.

## What we don't know

Nothing we found gives the experiment designs, the number of samples, how long each runs, or how results will be shared. A week-long visit limits what can be measured aboard, so much of the real analysis would happen after seeds and samples return to Earth.

## Why this matters for judging the mission

A first flight earns headlines for the milestone, and it is fair to ask whether the science justifies the seat. Algae and seed studies are modest, well-understood experiment types, which is reasonable for a short visit by a new astronaut. The test of the program will be whether SUPARCO publishes methods and results, and whether Pakistani researchers get data they can build on. Until then, the experiments are described, not demonstrated.
