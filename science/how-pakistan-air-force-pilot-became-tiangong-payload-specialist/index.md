# How a Pakistan Air Force pilot trained to fly to Tiangong in 7 months

> Zeeshan Ali and Khurram Daud both flew for Pakistan before China picked them for its space program. Only one made Shenzhou-24, after a training run shorter than usual.

By InnotechInsider Staff · Science · Nov 27, 2026
Canonical URL: https://innotechinsider.com/science/how-pakistan-air-force-pilot-became-tiangong-payload-specialist/

When Muhammad Zeeshan Ali flew to Tiangong on November 25, he arrived as the end product of a pipeline that started less than two years earlier. The route from Pakistani air force pilot to a payload specialist on a Chinese spacecraft is short on precedent, so it is worth walking through what is actually known.

## The agreement

The program began with a paper. In February 2025, Pakistan's space agency SUPARCO and the China Manned Space Agency, known as CMSA, [signed a cooperation agreement](https://www.globalsecurity.org/space/library/news/2025/space-250228-globaltimes01.htm) in Islamabad covering the selection and training of Pakistani astronauts for flights to Tiangong. Under the deal, two Pakistanis would train in China and one would fly, as Dawn describes it.

## Selection

CMSA said it would run Pakistan's candidates through the same three-stage process it uses for Chinese astronauts: a preliminary round in Pakistan, then secondary and final rounds in China. Two candidates were picked: Ali and Khurram Daud, both serving Pakistan Air Force pilots, per [Dawn's report on the shortlist](https://www.dawn.com/news/1971638). Reports differ on exactly when they were named, with some pointing to February 2026 and others to April, so we are not giving a date.

Both went to the Astronaut Center of China in Beijing as reserve astronauts. China did not say which of the two would fly until November 24, the day before launch. No source we found calls Daud a backup in so many words. He was the other candidate, and the flight went to Ali.

## Seven months of training

The standard course at the Astronaut Center runs at least a year. Ali's was compressed to about seven months, according to Dawn. The curriculum, as reported, covered spacecraft systems, emergency procedures, experiment operations, centrifuge and survival training, and technical Chinese aerospace terms. Dawn adds that it included intensive Chinese-language classes, along with learning Chinese songs, reading poetry, calligraphy and table tennis, which says something about how much of the work was linguistic and cultural.

## Why payload specialist

A payload specialist is not a career astronaut who commands the spacecraft. The role is built around the experiments. That fits a one-week visit, and it fits the fact that both candidates came from flying backgrounds rather than from the Chinese astronaut corps. On Shenzhou-24, Tang Hongbo commands, and Ali's job is the science carried up for Pakistan and China.

## What to take from it

The compressed schedule suggests China adapted its process to fit a first foreign flight rather than running a foreign candidate through the full standard track. Whether it repeats the model for other countries, or only for close partners, is not something any of the coverage answers. For Pakistan, the immediate gain is a trained astronaut and a working channel into China's space program. What that channel carries next is the open question.
