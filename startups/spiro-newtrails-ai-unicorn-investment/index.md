# Spiro Hits $1.4B Valuation on Fresh $320M NewTrails Round

> Edge-inference startup Spiro secured $320 million in Series C capital led by NewTrails. The round pushes the robotics silicon designer into unicorn status.

By InnotechInsider Staff · Startups · Jun 22, 2026
Canonical URL: https://innotechinsider.com/startups/spiro-newtrails-ai-unicorn-investment/

> **TL;DR** Zurich-based edge silicon startup Spiro has closed a $320 million Series C led by NewTrails Investment Partners, valuing the firm at $1.4 billion as demand surges for localized physical-intelligence compute.

Spiro Technologies secured a $1.4 billion post-money valuation this morning after closing a $320 million Series C financing round. The capital injection was led by Singapore-headquartered growth firm NewTrails Investment Partners, with follow-on participation from Highland Horizon and institutional funds out of Munich and Tokyo.

The transaction marks the largest private capital injection into an embodied-intelligence semiconductor venture this quarter, propelling Spiro past the unicorn threshold less than three years after its stealth departure.

### The Capital Stack and Terms

The financing follows a fast-moving syndicate process triggered by Spiro's production tape-out of its Orion-3 micro-architecture late last month. According to regulatory disclosures filed with the [U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission Form D](https://www.sec.gov) registry, the Series C combines $280 million in primary equity with $40 million in equipment-backed venture debt.

| Round | Date | Capital Raised | Lead Investor | Post-Money Valuation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Seed | March 2024 | $12M | Alpine Forward | $38M |
| Series A | February 2025 | $48M | Horizon Venture Labs | $190M |
| Series B | November 2025 | $110M | Highland Horizon | $550M |
| Series C | October 2026 | $320M | NewTrails Investment Partners | $1,400M |

NewTrails managing partner Lina Tan will take a board seat as part of the transaction. Spiro plans to deploy the proceeds directly into wafer procurement at foundries in Dresden and Tainan, buffering its physical inventory against tight sub-3nm production queues.

### Moving AI Out of the Data Center

Spiro's core thesis centers on a stark mechanical bottleneck: autonomous equipment cannot rely on centralized hyperscaler clusters when sub-millisecond physical reactions are mandatory. Instead of training massive multi-modal transformers in server farms, Spiro builds specialized silicon based on [neuromorphic engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuromorphic_engineering) principles that execute pre-trained vision-action models on less than 15 watts.

That architectural focus has placed Spiro in direct competition with entrenched incumbent chipmakers, yet industrial customers are gravitating toward specialized hardware that bypasses cooling overhead. The company reports that over 40 enterprise clients across agricultural automation, warehouse logistics, and medical robotics are piloting its current-generation evaluation modules. As institutional allocators shift focus inside  pipelines, capital is moving away from generic consumer software applications toward capital-intensive physical stacks.

"Industrial OEMs are exhausted by latency spikes and massive power budgets," said Spiro chief executive Marcus Vance during an investor briefing earlier today. "Autonomous machines need self-contained reflex loops. You cannot pilot a two-ton combine harvester or an orthopedic surgical arm through an API call to Northern Virginia."

### The Road to Volume Production

The funding gives Spiro the runway required to ramp commercial volume through late 2027. Yet capital abundance does not entirely shield the company from supply constraints. Sourcing specialized packaging substrate remains a persistent bottleneck across the European hardware ecosystem, and any slippage in foundry timelines could delay its targeted Q2 2027 customer shipping window.

Spiro also faces intensifying pressure as established players build custom neural engines directly into general-purpose microcontrollers, intensifying competitive dynamics across the broader  sector.

For now, NewTrails has placed a definitive wager that specialized edge silicon will capture the industrial fleet before generic alternatives catch up. Spiro's balance sheet is fully armed to prove that bet right.
