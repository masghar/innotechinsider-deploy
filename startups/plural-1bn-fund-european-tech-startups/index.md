# Plural Targets €1 Billion Fund to Anchor European Tech Ambitions

> Venture firm Plural is raising a landmark €1 billion fund to back European founders, signaling deeper institutional faith in the continent's frontier tech.

By InnotechInsider Staff · Startups · Nov 16, 2026
Canonical URL: https://innotechinsider.com/startups/plural-1bn-fund-european-tech-startups/

> **TL;DR** Plural is in the process of raising a €1 billion investment vehicle, according to public reporting, substantially expanding its firepower to support European tech companies from early stages through large growth rounds.

Plural, the investment platform launched by experienced European operators, is assembling a €1 billion fund aimed at backing high-conviction technology companies across the continent, as first reported by the *Financial Times*.

The planned fund represents a notable step up in size for the firm, which closed a €250 million debut fund in 2022 followed by a €400 million sophomore vehicle in early 2024. If closed at the target amount, the vehicle would stand among the largest single pools of capital raised by an independent, operator-led investment firm in Europe.

### Scaling the Operator-First Thesis

Founded by Wise co-founder Taavet Hinrikus and Songkick co-founder Ian Hogarth—who also chairs the UK's AI Safety Institute, as documented by [gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/)—alongside Sten Tamkivi, Khaled Helioui, and Carina Namih, Plural pitched itself as a corrective to traditional European venture capital. The founders argued that professional money managers often lack the direct operational scars required to advise early-stage founders navigating hard technical hurdles.

The firm's rapid succession of fund raises highlights how institutional limited partners have warmed to operator-led strategies across [startups](/startups/) sectors that require deep domain fluency.

| Fund Generation | Vintage / Target | Fund Size | Core Deployment Focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plural Fund I | 2022 | €250 million | Early-stage, frontier tech, climate |
| Plural Fund II | 2024 | €400 million | AI infrastructure, applied science |
| Plural Fund III (Targeted) | 2026 | €1.0 billion | Deep tech, platform scale, sovereign capability |

Where Plural's earlier vehicles focused heavily on lead tickets between €1 million and €10 million, managing a ten-figure pool necessarily broadens its mandate. Public reporting indicates the new vehicle will allow the partnership to write much larger checks into breakout companies, helping them bypass the traditional growth squeeze that often forces European founders to look abroad for expansion rounds.

### Countering the Growth-Stage Deficit

European technology ecosystems have generated strong technical talent and initial seed traction, but mature startups frequently face a capital vacuum at Series B and beyond. Historically, late-stage funding rounds have been dominated by Silicon Valley institutions or Asian sovereign wealth funds, sometimes precipitating corporate relocations to Delaware or Singapore.

Initiatives tracked by [Invest Europe](https://www.investeurope.eu) have repeatedly highlighted this structural gap in domestic late-stage risk capital. At the same time, policy bodies like the [European Innovation Council](https://eic.ec.europa.eu/) have pushed to keep strategic intellectual property anchored locally, particularly in critical infrastructure, defense tech, and domestic compute capabilities.

Plural's expanding vehicle reflects an appetite among institutional allocators to support domestic companies building sovereign [future tech](/future-tech/) architectures, from commercial space hardware to sovereign foundation models, without surrendering ownership to foreign syndicates at the first inflection point.

### The Execution Challenge at Ten Figures

Deploying €1 billion introduces distinct operational mechanics. Plural’s core brand premise rests on partners taking an active, hands-on role with only a small number of portfolio companies per year. Maintaining that high-touch dynamic becomes mathematically difficult as capital under management swells, prompting the firm to either expand its partner bench or systematically scale its ticket sizes into capital-intensive sectors.

The move also comes at a time when enterprise software spending faces closer balance-sheet scrutiny across global [biz it](/biz-it/) divisions, shifting venture attention toward defensive moats, physical hardware integration, and hard science.

Closing a fund of this scale would mark a coming-of-age test for Plural. The hurdle is no longer proving that founders make compelling early-stage backers, but showing that an operator-run collective can deploy institutional-scale growth capital while maintaining top-quartile returns.
