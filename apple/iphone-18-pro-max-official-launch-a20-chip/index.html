iPhone 18 Pro and Pro Max: Apple's 2nm Gamble Finally Ships

Apple's first 2nm iPhone chip, a variable-aperture camera, and a new CEO at the keynote podium. Here's everything that actually shipped in the iPhone 18 Pro and Pro Max.

MV
Marcus Vale

Sep 15, 2026, 6:18 AM UTC · 4 min read

TL;DR — Apple’s iPhone 18 Pro and Pro Max launched September 9 with the A20 Pro, the company’s first 2nm chip, plus a variable-aperture main camera and meaningfully better sustained performance. Prices start at $1,199 and $1,299. It was also the first iPhone keynote delivered by a CEO other than Tim Cook in fifteen years.

Apple’s “Surprise and shine” event on September 9 was billed as an iPhone launch, and it delivered one — but the biggest surprise wasn’t a spec sheet. It was who stood on stage to present it. John Ternus, promoted to chief executive on September 1 after Tim Cook moved to executive chairman, delivered his first product keynote as CEO — a changing of the guard that arguably matters more to Apple’s next decade than anything in the iPhone 18 Pro’s spec sheet.

Still, the phones themselves are a real generational jump, anchored by a chip Apple has been building toward for years.

The A20 Pro: Apple’s First 2-Nanometer Chip

Every iPhone Pro chip upgrade gets called a big deal; this one actually is. The A20 Pro is fabricated on a 2-nanometer process — a manufacturing milestone Apple has been racing TSMC toward — and pairs a new six-core CPU with a seven-core GPU and a dual 16-core Neural Engine. Apple says memory bandwidth is up 50% over the A19 Pro, a number that matters more for on-device Apple Intelligence workloads than for gaming benchmarks, per GSMArena’s hands-on report.

The smaller process node also freed up thermal headroom. Apple paired the A20 Pro with a larger vapor chamber and new chip packaging borrowed from its M-series Mac silicon, claiming up to 40% better sustained performance than the iPhone 17 Pro under sustained load — the kind of number that matters far more for someone recording 4K video for twenty minutes than for a quick benchmark run.

smartphone camera lens macro shot with aperture blades — Photo by Marcos Paulo Prado on Unsplash

A Camera That Physically Changes Its Aperture

The headline hardware change is the new 48MP Fusion Main camera, which uses a genuine variable aperture — a first for iPhone. It mechanically shifts between f/1.48, f/1.8, f/2.8, and f/4 depending on the scene, rather than simulating depth-of-field changes in software after the fact. Apple says the widest setting delivers roughly 50% better low-light performance than a fixed aperture would allow.

Apple also opened up manual control to match: new Pro camera controls expose aperture, shutter speed, white balance, and a live histogram directly in the Camera app, pushing the iPhone further into territory previously reserved for dedicated mirrorless cameras and third-party pro camera apps.

Both models also shrink the Dynamic Island slightly compared to the iPhone 17 Pro generation, continuing Apple’s slow march toward hiding the front camera and Face ID sensors entirely — a goal that, notably, the company’s new foldable iPhone Duo partially achieves with an under-display front camera instead.

The Bigger Story: Apple Just Split Its iPhone Lineup in Two

Here’s the part that didn’t get a slide at the keynote: Apple isn’t releasing a standard iPhone 18 this month at all. The iPhone 18, a new budget iPhone 18e, and an iPhone Air 2 are all being held back until spring 2027, according to MacRumors’ event coverage. September is now a Pro-and-foldable-only event; the mainstream and budget lineup gets its own launch window six months later.

That’s a real change to how Apple has released iPhones for over a decade, and it’s not hard to guess why: with the A20 Pro on a brand-new 2nm process and a $2,000 foldable launching in the same quarter, spreading supply-chain risk and marketing attention across two events rather than cramming everything into one September keynote makes practical sense — even if it means iPhone 18 (non-Pro) buyers have a longer wait ahead of them.

Should You Upgrade?

If you’re on an iPhone 15 Pro or older, the case is straightforward: a real 2nm chip, meaningfully better sustained performance, and a camera with actual moving parts rather than software tricks add up to the most substantive Pro-line jump in a few generations. If you’re on an iPhone 17 Pro from last year, this is a more marginal call — most of what’s new here compounds gradually rather than in one dramatic leap, and the redesigned Siri AI experience in iOS 27 is available on your existing phone already.

Pre-orders opened September 12; the iPhone 18 Pro and Pro Max reached stores September 18.
