# Nearly one in five workers plan time off for GTA VI, CNBC reports

> GTA VI is moving PTO calendars, streaming forecasts and analyst models before it ships. The strongest claims are survey and consultancy projections, not sales data.

By InnotechInsider Staff · Gaming · Nov 18, 2026
Canonical URL: https://innotechinsider.com/gaming/gta-6-launch-pto-streaming-economic-ripple/

Grand Theft Auto VI has not been out for a day in most of the world, and it is already showing up in places that have nothing to do with game charts. A [CNBC roundup of the launch's knock-on effects](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/14/gta-6-grand-theft-auto-launch.html) collects work calendars, food delivery, streaming and tourism into one picture. Most of it is projection, and it is worth separating the measured from the guessed.

## Time off is the one hard-ish number

The most concrete figure comes from HR software company BambooHR, which told CNBC that paid-time-off requests for the release date are nearly 13% above the equivalent days in 2024 and 2025. The same report says nearly one in five workers plan to take time off for the launch. That is a survey and request-data signal from one vendor's customer base, not a count of everyone in the country, so read it as direction rather than size.

The game itself launches November 19 on PS5 and Xbox Series X|S, with [no PC release for now](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/live/news/grand-theft-auto-6-live-launch-coverage/), and digital buyers could preload from [November 10](https://www.rockstargames.com/newswire/article/k4ak9okk485712/grand-theft-auto-vi-pre-load-begins-on-november-10), according to Rockstar's own announcement. (Some earlier pre-order listings had shown November 12, which Rockstar later corrected.) Preloading matters here: it lets people who took the day off spend it playing instead of downloading.

## Sales projections, with the source attached

CNBC cites Sensor Tower's estimate of more than 12 million preorders through November 13, and Morgan Stanley's forecast of 40 million copies sold by March 2027. Both are third-party estimates. Roth analyst Eric Handler separately estimated that Rockstar spent more than $1 billion developing the game, again an analyst's estimate and not a company disclosure.

Take-Two chief executive Strauss Zelnick told CNBC that "our expectations were high and they are being vastly exceeded so far." He gave no figures in the quoted passage, so that is sentiment, not data.

## The side effects are mostly forecasts

PwC projected a surge in food delivery orders and a lift for Miami tourism. As a comparison point, PwC cited a 118% rise in foreign visitors to Seoul's Bukchon Hanok Village in the four weeks after *KPop Demon Hunters* debuted. That is a different kind of media event, and the comparison shows how much of the tourism case rests on analogy.

The sharper warning came from PwC's Bart Spiegel, who said: "You're going to start to pull eyeballs away from video streaming platforms." He expects analysts in the first quarter of 2027 to cite game-related declines in some companies' results. Twitch's chief product officer, Mike Minton, took the opposite view for his own industry, saying the opportunity "goes beyond this launch moment."

## What to watch

The first real test is not the sales estimates. It is whether streaming platforms and delivery apps report anything unusual for launch week when they next release numbers, and whether the PTO bump shows up in attendance data rather than requests. Requests are cheap. Actual absences, and actual viewing hours lost, are what would make the "GTA effect" a measured phenomenon instead of a well-timed press narrative. Until then, the safest reading is that demand is clearly enormous and the downstream economics are still guesswork.
