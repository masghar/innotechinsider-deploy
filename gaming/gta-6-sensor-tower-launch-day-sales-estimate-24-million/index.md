# Sensor Tower puts GTA VI at 24.6M copies, but Take-Two hasn't confirmed

> A tracker says GTA VI sold about 24.6 million copies and $2.36 billion by launch day. It's an estimate with real gaps, and Take-Two hasn't confirmed a number.

By InnotechInsider Staff · Gaming · Nov 20, 2026
Canonical URL: https://innotechinsider.com/gaming/gta-6-sensor-tower-launch-day-sales-estimate-24-million/

Nobody outside Take-Two can say how many copies of *Grand Theft Auto VI* sold on day one, because Take-Two hasn't said. The number everyone is quoting, roughly 24.6 million copies and $2.36 billion, comes from a market-research firm's tracker, and it needs to be read as exactly that.

## What Sensor Tower is estimating

Market intelligence firm Sensor Tower [estimates](https://www.notebookcheck.net/GTA-6-passes-2-36-billion-in-estimated-launch-day-sales.1455216.0.html) that GTA VI earned about $2.36 billion from about 24.6 million copies by the end of its November 19 launch day. TweakTown, which cites the same tracker, [puts the unit count at 24.58 million](https://www.tweaktown.com/news/114795/gta-6-day-one-sales-estimates-break-24-5-million-with-dollars2-36-billion-earned/index.html).

Notebookcheck reports the figure covers digital purchases only, and that it includes pre-orders. That matters for how you read "launch day." Much of that total was bought before the game unlocked, so it is closer to a cumulative tally through launch than a count of people who bought on Thursday.

Per Notebookcheck's account of the tracker, the split was lopsided:

- **PlayStation 5:** about 18.44 million copies, worth $1.77 billion
- **Xbox Series X|S:** about 6.15 million copies, worth $588 million

That works out to roughly 75% of copies on PlayStation. The same tracker's pre-order trajectory, as Notebookcheck lays it out, passed $1 billion from about 10.31 million units eight days before release and reached $1.84 billion from about 19.1 million copies by Thursday morning. Most of the total was already banked before launch day began.

## What the estimate can't tell you

Three caveats are worth holding onto.

First, TweakTown notes that Sensor Tower's figures do not include refunds. Take-Two's CEO Strauss Zelnick has himself cautioned shareholders that pre-orders can be refunded, so the tracker's total is a ceiling on what stays sold, not a final count.

Second, physical copies are outside the estimate. Notebookcheck says the numbers exclude them, so a retail-heavy market would be undercounted even as refunds push the digital figure the other way.

Third, neither article explains Sensor Tower's methodology beyond saying the numbers come from its sales tracker. Treat the platform split and the revenue figure with the same caution as the headline total.

## Why the official number will look different

Take-Two [kept its fiscal 2027 net bookings forecast](https://www.notebookcheck.net/GTA-6-passes-2-36-billion-in-estimated-launch-day-sales.1455216.0.html) at $8 billion to $8.2 billion, and president Karl Slatoff called pre-orders "extraordinary" on the second-quarter earnings call. Zelnick has said the company will share official sales data soon, with no date given in the coverage we reviewed.

Whenever it arrives, expect a different shape. Publishers usually report sell-in or sell-through on their own definitions, and revenue is not the same as bookings. TweakTown also points out that the $20 portion of the Ultimate Edition is treated as a digital good, similar to microtransactions, which is the sort of accounting detail that makes a third-party revenue estimate and a company's bookings figure hard to line up.

## The comparison to resist

Expect the headline "beats GTA V" to circulate. TweakTown says the $2.36 billion estimate is 76% above the $1.34 billion GTA V earned in its launch quarter. That compares a launch-day estimate that bundles in pre-orders with a full quarter of actual results, so it isn't a like-for-like record. We'd wait for Take-Two's own figures, with its own definitions, before calling anything a record.

The estimate does support one safe conclusion: the pre-order wave was enormous, and it was overwhelmingly digital and overwhelmingly on PlayStation. Everything finer than that waits on the company that has the real ledger.
