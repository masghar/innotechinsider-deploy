# Take-Two says GTA VI hit $3.79B on day one. Read the footnote first

> Take-Two's $3.79 billion day-one figure for GTA VI is a company estimate of retail spending, pre-orders included. It is not revenue, and not a single day of sales.

By InnotechInsider Staff · Gaming · Nov 22, 2026
Canonical URL: https://innotechinsider.com/gaming/gta-6-take-two-3-79-billion-day-one-retail-sales-explained/

Take-Two has finally put its own number on *Grand Theft Auto VI*'s launch, and it is a big one: $3.79 billion in day-one retail sales. The footnote attached to that figure matters more than the headline, because it changes what the number can be compared with.

## What Take-Two actually claimed

In [a November 20 press release](https://www.take2games.com/ir/news/grand-theft-auto-vi-shatters-entertainment-box-office-records), Take-Two and Rockstar called the launch "the biggest entertainment launch in history" and said GTA VI sold 32.5 million units before release, which they describe as a record across all platforms. The release also repeats the game's Metacritic score of 99.

The $3.79 billion carries an asterisk. The footnote says it is "based on company estimates through end of day November 19th worldwide." The release does not define "retail sales" any further.

## It is not one day of purchases

The figure includes pre-orders. [Make Tech Easier's rundown](https://maketecheasier.com/gta-6-day-one-sales-record/), which attributes that point to The Hollywood Reporter, says the $3.79 billion is not a single day of purchases. Months of pre-sales are folded into a number labelled "day one". That is a fair way to measure a launch, but it is not what most readers picture when they see the phrase.

It is also gross spending at the register, not Take-Two's revenue. Platform holders and storefronts take a cut of each sale, and the release does not say how much reaches Take-Two. Nor is it a profit figure, and nothing in the release is audited.

## How it squares with Sensor Tower

Our [earlier report on Sensor Tower's tracker](/gaming/gta-6-sensor-tower-launch-day-sales-estimate-24-million/) noted that Take-Two hadn't confirmed any number. It now has, and the two do not match. Sensor Tower estimated about $2.36 billion in pre-order revenue and roughly 24.5 million pre-orders for PlayStation and Xbox, according to Make Tech Easier, and our earlier report noted that tracker counted digital sales only. Take-Two's 32.5 million units are the company's own count, and the release does not break the figure down. GTA VI is only on those two consoles, so the gap is about what each method counts, not about extra platforms.

That is roughly a $1.4 billion gap, but the two figures measure different things, so it isn't a like-for-like miss. Different scope, different window, and one is an outside estimate while the other is the publisher's own. The sensible reading is that Sensor Tower's number was a floor, since it left out physical and other sales it could not track, not a forecast that failed.

## The GTA V comparison needs a caveat too

Make Tech Easier reports that CNBC called the new total "nearly five times" GTA V's roughly $800 million first-day figure from 2013, which works out to about 4.7x. That 2013 number was also a company estimate, and it excluded Japan and Brazil, which hadn't launched yet. Neither number is audited, and the two were assembled under different conditions, so treat the multiple as a headline, not a measurement.

## What to watch

The cleaner test is Take-Two's next earnings report, where net bookings and unit data will be reported on the company's own accounting basis. Until then, $3.79 billion is best read as a confident marketing claim built on a real record: 32.5 million units sold before anyone could play the game. What it says about how much money Take-Two keeps, we don't know yet.
